The page is white; exactly two sections carry a wash, chosen by name in App.jsx (SECTION_TINT), never by position. Salads → --band-mint (.shk-app__section--tint); Coffee → --band-warm (.shk-app__section--warm), which is --cream-50 off the brand warm-white ramp, so drinks change key without a second green. Striping every other section was tried and read as a barcode. Both bands are edge-to-edge — on wide screens they break out of the centred column with margin-inline: calc(50% - 50vw) and put the width back as padding. The colour change is the only divider: a straight edge, no hairline and no shaped one. Dish photos are transparent cut-outs and carry NO cast shadow. They used to sit on a periwinkle drop-shadow(-5px 11px 9px var(--produce-shadow)) to fake them floating off the page; on the light theme that read as grime under the food rather than lift, so it was removed — the food sits flat on the card. If a photo ever needs lifting again it is drop-shadow, never box-shadow, so the cast traces the silhouette and not a rectangle. The fruit/veg slices are drawn as SVG, not photographed — one drawing serves every size — and all 59 live in lib/fruitArt.jsx, shared by both surfaces that use them (see Fruit below).

Section Art (gutter cut-outs)

Above 1200px the card grid stops growing and the page opens an empty white column either side of it. .shk-sec-art drops one oversized dish photograph into that dead space — the printed menu's own trick, and the only element allowed to break the container. Two shapes, two treatments: a roughly square dish is blown up past the viewport edge and cropped by it; a column-shaped dish (a spring roll) is shown whole and tall instead.

the grid ends here →
art starts in the gutter
Which sections get art, and on which side, is decided in App.jsx (SECTION_ART) by name — not here and not by position. Three or four over the whole menu is the ceiling: the effect works because it is rare, and past that the gutter stops being a surprise and becomes wallpaper. Sides alternate down the scroll. Rules the component must keep: · It lives ONLY in the gutter, never under a filled grid. Cards are transparent cut-outs with no tile chrome, so anything sliding behind them shows through the gaps as stray fragments of food. The anchor is measured from the section's centre line — left: calc(50% + var(--content-max) / 2 - var(--art-inset, 20px)) — which is the viewport centre for a contained section AND for a full-bleed tinted one, so one rule serves both. · --art-inset is how far the art may reach back INSIDE the content column. Default 20px = just kissing the edge. Raise it ONLY where that side of the section is genuinely empty: Potato Tacos uses 200px because the tiers layout stops ~190px short of the right edge, which lets the big taco sit beside the small ones and show ~70% of itself instead of half. Never raise it over a full grid. Set per section in App.jsx (SECTION_ART.inset); measure the clearance, don't guess. · SQUARE-ISH ART (default, aspect-ratio 1): width is gutter + 420px of deliberate overspill, capped at 980px, so the photo reads as cropped by the page rather than parked beside it. It widens with the window instead of being pinned to a size. The overspill sets the SCALE, not the footprint — the visible slice is still only the gutter (plus any --art-inset), so a wider box shows less dish, larger. Potato Tacos and Salads are this kind. · COLUMN-SHAPED ART (--art-w + --art-ratio, set per section in App.jsx): overspill is geometrically impossible here. A spring roll is 0.26 wide-to-tall, so widening the box until it reached the viewport edge would make it thousands of pixels tall, and cropping its right side would slice the roll open lengthwise instead of trimming a margin. Such art is shown WHOLE and tall in the gutter: --art-w sizes it (gutter + inset, capped — Fresh Spring Roll caps at 470px, roughly the 900px asset's limit before it upscales) and --art-ratio replaces the square default so the height follows the file. At ultrawide the cap leaves clean gutter to the right of the art; that is correct, not a gap to fill. · .shk-sec-art--top (App.jsx: align:"top") is mandatory for column art. The default anchor centres the box on the section's middle, and a 1808px-tall roll centred on a 490px section shows only its middle — an anonymous wall of shrimp with both ends off-screen. --top pins it to the section's top edge (translate 0 -140px) so the guest meets the roll's cut end, where the fresh veg and mango are, and the rest runs off down the page. · .shk-sec-art--under (App.jsx: align:"under") is for art that owns half the CONTENT column rather than a gutter crop — today only Salads, whose section carries .shk-app__section--split (--cols-wide:2, grid pushed to margin-inline-start:50%, title raised to z-index 1). That art is the second thing on the page after the heading, so centring it on an 1800px band left a hole above it and read as unrelated to the title. --under pins it just below the heading (top: var(--art-top,104px), translate reset to 0 because top is now an absolute anchor, not a centre line) so the eye runs Salads → bowl → dishes. --art-top is the escape hatch if a heading ever gets taller. · NO second decoration in the gutter. Circular cut-outs of real produce photography (.shk-sec-produce) were built to sit alongside the art and were cut after review: beside dish photographs they read as stickers stuck to the margin and pulled the eye off the food. One big photograph of an actual dish per section, nothing orbiting it. The site's fruit lives in exactly two places — the hero scatter and the falling sliceable layer, both vector (lib/fruitArt.jsx). Do not add a third. · Tall art overhangs its neighbours by hundreds of pixels, so it cannot be signed off by eye at one scroll position. Sweep it in DOCUMENT space (add scroller.scrollTop, not viewport rects) against .shk-card__media, .shk-card__name, .shk-seal, .shk-mana__item, .shk-set, .shk-rows li and .shk-app__sec-title over the whole page; the answer must be zero overlaps. · The file is attached as background:var(--art) INSIDE @media (min-width:1440px) — a phone never downloads it. An <img> here would cost every mobile guest ~200 KB of decoration they cannot see. 1440 is also the first width where the gutter reads as a photograph rather than a sliver. · aria-hidden + pointer-events:none. It is scenery, never a target, and never the only place a dish appears. · Same shadow law as the cards: transparent cut-out, no cast shadow. Assets: transparent WebP, alpha-trimmed, in public/assets/section/ — size the dimension the box actually constrains: square art to a longest edge of 1200, column art to a WIDTH of 900 (springroll-shrimp.webp is 900x3467, 196 KB, ratio 0.260; capping the longest edge instead would have starved it to ~310px wide and left it soft). · src/styles/components.css

Fruit

Fifty-nine produce cut-outs, drawn as SVG in lib/fruitArt.jsx — one source, two surfaces: the still scatter behind the hero copy (FruitConfetti.jsx) and the falling, sliceable layer over the whole site (FruitFall.jsx). Every slice is authored in a 100×100 box as a circle centred on (50,50) — the top-down "cut through the middle" view. That's not just a style rule: it's why the falling layer can treat any item as a circle of radius 50 for hit detection without special-casing a single one.

The library is one table, PRODUCE, where each row declares everything about an item at once — { kind, score, juice, art, scale?, w? } — and the flat maps (SLICE_ART, JUICE, SCORE, SCALE, KIND) are derived from it. With sixty items, four hand-maintained parallel maps would drift within a week. Four families: fruit (citrus, pome, stone, berry, melon, tropical), veg, nut and seed. Families share drawing templates — citrus(), pome(), stone(), gourd(), capsicum(), beads() — so a new orange variety is one line, not forty paths.

Nuts and seeds are drawn to fill the same 100×100 box and shrunk at spawn with scale instead of being given a custom hit box. One hit rule survives, and the small items become genuinely harder to hit — which is what their higher scores are paying for. The chili uses the same trick against the bell pepper: identical template, half the size.

A fruit is the same drawing rendered twice, each copy clipped to one half (inset(0 49.7% 0 0) / inset(0 0 0 49.7%) — the 0.3% overlap kills a hairline seam on fractional DPRs). Whole, the halves sit on top of each other and read as one piece; cutting only moves them apart. The cut is aligned by rotating their shared parent to swipe − 90° − spin, and the drawing inside each half counter-rotates by the same amount — so the split follows the finger but the fruit never appears to snap round when it's hit.

Calories

The counter adds up the calories the guest actually burns swiping. There is no goal, no prize and nothing to win — it is a toy, and it touches the order in no way at all.

Each row's score is an effort rating — how hard the item is to hit (lemon 60, pomegranate 300, cashew 520, a scatter of chia 1100). Calories are derived from it in one place, state/slicer.jsx, so retuning difficulty can never desync from the number on screen. Spawn weights (w) keep the rare, high-effort items rare; pickProduce() walks a cumulative weight table, so tuning rarity is editing one number in one place.

The numbers are small because that is what a swipe costs, and this is the one rule here worth defending. Every dish on this site publishes a real calorie count. A counter claiming the guest burned thousands while browsing would sit on the same screen as those figures and quietly make a liar of them — a kitchen selling honest food should not be the one place a customer meets invented nutrition maths. Small and true is funnier than big and made up: "you have burned 0.4 calories" is the joke, and it only works if the 0.4 is real.

The counter (SliceScore.jsx, bottom-left, mirroring the Order button opposite) does not exist until you have cut something. Nobody arrives at a restaurant menu wanting a game explained to them. A "PLAY!" badge on a menu is an ad; a counter that appears after you have already played is a wink.

Counter state/slicer.jsx — localStorage "shk.slicer.v3" (v2 held credits + a discount; both removed) kcal = 0.02 × √(effort ÷ 60) → easiest slice 0.020, hardest 0.086, average ≈ 0.038 Why a root, not a multiplier: effort spans 18× because it rates how hard a thing is to HIT. The energy cost of the swipes does not — a hard slice is a faster, longer arm movement, but it is still one arm movement. Scaling linearly would keep the ordering and lose the physics (chia would "burn" half a calorie in a single flick). The root keeps the ordering, compresses the spread to ~4×, and leaves both ends defensible out loud. 0.02 kcal ≈ a half-second arm movement at roughly 3 kcal/min above resting. Display total: 2 decimals under 1 cal, 1 decimal above — early on a single slice IS most of the total and one decimal would show a stubborn 0.0. Pops: always 2 decimals, so "+0.1" never looks like a bug. Two tabs share one key, so writes merge rather than overwrite and the total stays monotonic — it only goes up. A `storage` listener lets an idle tab catch up instead of showing a stale number. NO DISCOUNT. The counter feeds nothing: the cart does not read it and no price anywhere depends on it. If a promotion is ever wanted, it belongs in the POS with server-issued codes, not in a number the guest can edit from devtools in thirty seconds. SLICE_ART (59 items) · SCORE · SCALE · KIND · ART_KEYS · JUICE · pickProduce() · FruitSymbols — all lib/fruitArt.jsx, one <symbol> per item, rendered once per page and referenced by <use>. That indirection is what lets the falling layer respawn a slot as a different item by swapping one href, with no React render. Anti-patterns

Hard "no" list — enforced on every PR (full checklist in design-system/MASTER.md).

  • ❌ Emojis as icons → use the SVG set in components/Icons.jsx
  • ❌ outline:none without the global focus shadow
  • ❌ Color-only meaning (always pair an icon/text — diets, allergens, macros)
  • ❌ Off-brand fonts (Playfair/Karla) or raw hex in components — use tokens
  • ❌ Layout-shifting hovers; instant (0ms) state changes
  • ❌ --text-on-dark-faint on small text (fails AA below 24px)
